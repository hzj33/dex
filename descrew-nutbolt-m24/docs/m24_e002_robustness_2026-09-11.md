## Evidence and hypothesis

[DemoStart III-A](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06613v1) treats starting states as task parameters and evaluates on a target distribution. [Solving Rubik's Cube with a Robot Hand](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07113) uses domain randomization to improve dexterous-policy robustness. Our local adaptation samples bounded independent uniform errors in active starting joints and keeps a50%nominal mixture during training. This does not reproduce either paper's automatic curriculum/randomization algorithm, and the chosen one-degree range is task-specific diagnostic evidence, not a paper formula.

Original-model five-cycle baselines,128environments per condition: nominal128, +/-0.5degree101, +/-1degree66, +/-2degrees43 at seed42; +/-1degree seeds43/44 yield70/76. The one-degree condition gives both success and failure signal. Independent noisy batches contain128 unique initial offsets with inactive-finger offsets zero. The initial one-degree seed42 batch has37 failures before completing two cycles and25 later failures.

First hypothesis: additional PPO on these starts improves robustness with unchanged rewards. The first comparison retained two-cycle training and failed: nominal and aggregate perturbed results regressed. LAST had28 of32 nominal-five-cycle failures in the third drive/release, beyond its two-cycle training horizon. This motivates one controlled follow-up: start again from the original model and extend the training horizon to five cycles, matching the evaluated behavior. The follow-up improves both tested perturbation groups and preserves nominal performance. It supports the horizon hypothesis in this comparison; it is not a multi-seed training study or proof of a universal cause.
