# M24 E002 Human Like Two Finger Twist Training Plan

Date: 2026-09-04  
Branch: `feat/m24-humanlike-twist`  
Task path: `XHandPasiniM24NutBolt -> PPO teacher -> evaluation`

## 1. Decision

E001 is rejected. E002 will use a skill-decomposed curriculum rather than
training rotation, release, return, and regrasp in one reward from the start.
The final target remains a repeated human-like pinch-twist cycle:

1. index and thumb both contact and both contribute in the commanded direction;
2. the nut makes monotonic net angular progress during a short drive stroke;
3. index and thumb release on the same 20 Hz policy step and remain off briefly;
4. both fingers return without touching or reversing the nut;
5. both fingers recontact together and begin the next stroke.

E002 has three training stages. E002-A learns one valid drive stroke, E002-B
learns one complete drive-release-return-regrasp cycle, and E002-C learns
repeated cycles with progressively restored randomization. No later stage may
start from a checkpoint that failed the previous stage's fixed-step evaluation.

## 2. What the research changes

The supplied review is useful in six ways:

- use contact as a gate on task progress, not as a positive reward that can be
  accumulated while holding still;
- clip and normalize angular progress, and pay a milestone only on its first
  crossing;
- decompose grasp, drive, release, and return rather than asking one
  unconditioned policy to infer an invisible phase;
- introduce stability costs only after useful motion exists;
- use success-conditioned curricula and reset-state coverage instead of
  opening all randomization and all costs at a fixed training step;
- select checkpoints by task metrics and event validity, not episodic return.

The following review suggestions are intentionally not used in the immediate
E002 reward:

- motor current is deferred by user decision;
- force-overload reward is deferred until the fingertip resultant-force proxy
  is calibrated and separated from non-nut contacts;
- CoP and taxel-derived slip cannot be assumed because the current Isaac Gym
  actor/task does not expose a calibrated DexH13 taxel representation;
- axis and axial-pitch rewards are not meaningful for the current fixed-axis,
  revolute-only nut asset;
- published numerical weights are not copied across different normalization,
  control rates, hands, or contact models.

Primary references for the design direction are SD-RL for skill decomposition,
Tac2Motion for contact-gated rotation and gait events, DexScrew for the
fixed-axis primitive, AnyRotate for clipped progress and staged regularization,
and PenSpin/DemoStart for reset-state curricula.

- SD-RL: `https://doi.org/10.1360/SST-2025-0295`
- Tac2Motion: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17812`
- DexScrew: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02011`
- AnyRotate: `https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/yang25c.html`
- PenSpin: `https://proceedings.mlr.press/v270/wang25j.html`
- DemoStart: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06613`

## 4. Required implementation before training

### 4.1 M24-only phase command

The current actor sees 96 values consisting of three frames of hand joint
position and target history. E001's internal reward phase is not observable by
the actor, which makes drive, release, and return ambiguous at the same hand
state.

Add an optional M24-only actor-observation tail containing:

- a four-value one-hot command for `drive`, `release`, `return`, `regrasp`;
- one normalized elapsed-phase value in `[0, 1]`.

E002-A uses the same observation shape with the command fixed to `drive`.
E002-B and E002-C can therefore resume the accepted A checkpoint without
changing network input shape. The command is generated by a deterministic
external gait supervisor and is reproducible at deployment; simulator-only
contact force, nut angle, and phase-success truth remain privileged reward or
critic inputs and must not be appended to the actor observation.

The observation extension must be opt-in so historical 96-dimensional policies
and all other tasks remain loadable.

### 4.2 Correct progress state

For commanded direction `s` and nut joint position `theta`, maintain per
environment:

```text
x_t         = s * (theta_t - theta_start)
h_t         = max(h_(t-1), x_t)
h_paid_t    = min(h_t, theta_target)
delta_new   = max(h_paid_t - h_paid_(t-1), 0)
delta_step  = s * (theta_t - theta_(t-1))
delta_rev   = max(-delta_step, 0)
gross       = sum(abs(delta_step))
net         = x_T
```

Only `delta_new` may generate positive progress reward. Positive motion below
the previous high-water mark and overshoot beyond the stroke target receive
zero progress reward. Every reverse increment is penalized in every phase.
Reset all progress/high-water buffers on episode reset and verify them with
deterministic traces before GPU startup. Keep the uncapped high-water value for
diagnostics even though paid progress is target-capped.

### 4.3 Pair contribution gate

For index and thumb, retain the validated R1 contact hysteresis as an initial
contact detector:

```text
contact on:  force >= 0.30 and fingertip-nut distance <= 0.045 m
contact off: force <  0.15 or  fingertip-nut distance >  0.050 m
```

For finger `i`, compute a bounded contribution quality:

```text
c_i = min(force_confidence_i, distance_confidence_i)
t_i = clip(positive_signed_axial_torque_i / torque_saturation, 0, 1)
g_i = c_i * t_i
G_pair = min(g_index, g_thumb)
```

Large force must not increase `c_i` beyond one. Before training, use controlled
positive and negative hand motions to prove the nut-axis sign and
fingertip-force sign. Also log ungated net progress. If the resultant fingertip
force cannot distinguish nut contact from another collision, do not hide the
problem by increasing its scale; replace or validate the contact source first.

### 4.4 Phase-aware termination

The existing task resets when either active fingertip exceeds 0.05 m from the
nut, when nut motion is stagnant, or when nut contact disappears. Those rules
conflict with an intentional release phase.

E002 termination must be phase-aware:

- in `drive`, loss of pair contact or lack of progress by the deadline is a
  failed episode;
- in `release` and `return`, planned loss of contact and stationary nut motion
  are permitted, while either fingertip exceeding an initial 0.075 m
  nut-centre workspace remains failure;
- in `return`, either fingertip contacting the nut is an explicit failure;
- in `regrasp`, missing the double-contact deadline is failure;
- no timeout or termination may be counted as a phase success.

Every termination publishes exactly one reason code. This change must be
M24-E002-only so historical task behavior is preserved.

## 5. E002-A single monotonic pair-drive stroke

### 5.1 Objective

Learn one approximately 30 degree drive stroke from the saved stable initial
grasp. E002-A is not a release policy and must never be presented as the final
behavior.

### 5.2 Initial reward

All terms below are dimensionless and clipped to `[0, 1]` before weighting.
Use `delta_ref = 3 degrees` per 20 Hz policy step as the initial progress
normalizer.

```text
r_eff       = G_pair * clip(delta_new / delta_ref, 0, 1)
c_reverse   = clip(delta_rev / delta_ref, 0, 1)
r_approach  = change in pair-contact potential before first pair contact only
r_milestone = one-shot first crossing of 15 and 30 degrees
r_success   = first completion of the valid four-step target hold
c_overspeed = soft excess above 5 degrees per policy step
c_overshoot = soft excess above 35 degrees current net stroke

r_A = 4.0*r_eff - 4.0*c_reverse + 0.25*r_approach
      + 1.0*r_milestone + 10.0*r_success
      - 0.25*c_overspeed - 1.0*c_overshoot
```

`r_approach` is a potential difference and cannot pay for unchanged contact.
Set the legacy per-step proximity reward to zero. Do not include E001 phase
bonus, pose penalty, torque penalty, work penalty, force penalty, current
penalty, or per-step contact reward in the first A probe. Record those quantities
as diagnostics where available.

End a nominal A episode successfully only after the current net angle stays in
the `30 +/- 5` degree band with valid pair contribution for four steps and no
reverse step above 2 degrees. A deadline without the target is failure with
`-2`, never an automatic transition or success. A collision-like increment
above 15 degrees in one policy step terminates with `-10`.

### 5.3 Curriculum

Start with one object scale, the saved grasp, no object-position noise, fixed
PD gains, nominal mass/friction, and no random external force. After the nominal
gate passes, restore one randomization family at a time under a success-rate
gate:

1. initial nut/hand position noise up to 1 mm;
2. friction in a narrow range around nominal;
3. mass and centre-of-mass variation;
4. PD, observation/action noise, scale, and external force last.

Do not immediately restore the historical friction range `[0.5, 8.0]`. Expand
only while the previous validation set remains above its gate.

### 5.4 Acceptance

On a deterministic fixed-step evaluation of at least 512 environments:

- at least 90 percent reach 30 degrees in the nominal set and at least 85
  percent in the current randomization set;
- median net/gross efficiency is at least 0.90 and its P10 is at least 0.80;
- median reverse/gross is at most 0.05 and its P90 is at most 0.10;
- at least 95 percent of credited progress steps have valid index and thumb
  contribution; single-finger credited progress is at most 2 percent;
- collision-like hard-speed termination is below 1 percent;
- headed playback shows no repeated forward/backward oscillation.

## 6. E002-B one complete human-like cycle

Resume only an accepted E002-A checkpoint. Train exactly one complete cycle
before attempting long-horizon repetition.

### 6.1 Initial deterministic gait schedule

Use a fixed command schedule that can also be generated outside simulation.
Initial maximum durations at 20 Hz are:

- `drive`: 16 steps;
- `release`: 5 steps;
- `return`: 8 steps;
- `regrasp`: 5 steps.

These are command durations, not proof of success. At each phase boundary the
corresponding physical condition must be true or the episode fails. Change only
one duration at a time if traces show that a valid motion is physically clipped.

### 6.2 Valid events

- Drive succeeds only if the high-water angle enters `30 +/- 5` degrees with
  valid pair contribution. Reverse penalties remain active.
- Release succeeds only if index and thumb produce their first contact-off
  event on the same policy step and both remain off for two consecutive steps.
- Return requires both fingers to remain off. Reward only potential improvement
  toward the stored cycle-start joint/fingertip sector; unchanged or worsening
  pose earns nothing. Contact during return fails the episode.
- Regrasp succeeds only when both fingers produce contact-on on the same policy
  step in the valid regrasp sector and remain on for two steps.
- The cycle bonus is paid once only after all four events occur in order and net
  progress remains at least 25 degrees.

Initial event weights, after normalization, are `+2` for valid release, `+1`
for return-potential progress, `+2` for valid regrasp, and `+10` for a valid
cycle. An invalid phase boundary is `-5` and terminates. Continue using the A
progress and reverse terms; there is never a per-step contact reward.

Release lag is computed only for valid release events. A missing release has no
lag value and increments `invalid_release_count`; it must never enter an average
as zero.

### 6.3 Acceptance

- valid ordered one-cycle success rate at least 90 percent nominal and 80
  percent on the current randomized set;
- same-step double-release rate at least 95 percent of release attempts;
- two-step off-contact dwell at least 95 percent;
- same-step double-regrasp rate at least 90 percent;
- contact-during-return, timeout-promoted success, and continuous-contact cycle
  counts are exactly zero;
- reverse motion accumulated over release and return is at most 2 degrees per
  cycle;
- headed playback visibly shows both fingers drive, release, return off-contact,
  and regrasp.

If B fails two bounded probes because return/regrasp exploration is absent, do
not keep increasing event rewards. Use the existing pose tuner to record a
small set of released/pregrasp canonical states and add them as reset states or
potential targets, following the PenSpin/DemoStart reset-curriculum approach.

## 7. E002-C repeated cycles and robustness

Resume only an accepted E002-B checkpoint. Preserve the same reward definitions
and phase-command observation.

Advance through these success-gated curricula:

1. three valid cycles from the nominal saved pose;
2. six valid cycles and at least 180 degrees net angle;
3. command-duration jitter of plus or minus one policy step;
4. small random initial nut phase, then four to six validated initial
   grasp/release phase states;
5. restore friction, mass, centre-of-mass, PD/noise, scale, and external-force
   randomization one family at a time.

Use reset-state mixtures when exploration becomes sparse: whole-episode start,
drive endpoint, valid released state, and pregrasp state. Reset states assist
exploration; they do not themselves earn event or cycle bonuses.

Final E002-C acceptance on at least 512 environments requires:

- at least 80 percent complete six ordered cycles and at least 180 degrees net
  angle in the current randomized set;
- median net/gross at least 0.85 and median reverse/gross at most 0.08;
- pair-contributed progress at least 95 percent and single-finger credited
  progress at most 2 percent;
- valid same-step release at least 95 percent and regrasp at least 90 percent;
- zero timeout-promoted transitions and zero continuous-contact cycles;
- equivalent M24x3 virtual advance is positive and consistent with net angle;
- normal-real-time headed video shows at least six complete human-like cycles
  without forward/backward oscillation.

Only an accepted E002-C teacher may be considered for later load/force tuning,
student training, export, or sim-to-real deployment.

## 8. Metrics and checkpoint selection

Every training and evaluation rollout must report:

- unwrapped start, final, and high-water angle;
- new-high-water, reverse, gross, and net travel;
- net/gross and reverse/gross distributions, not only means;
- virtual M24x3 advance in millimetres;
- gated and ungated progress;
- per-finger contact confidence, signed torque, contribution quality, and the
  pair gate;
- pair-contributed and single-finger progress ratios;
- phase occupancy, valid phase transitions, and every failure-reason count;
- valid release/regrasp counts, release lag with valid sample count, off-contact
  dwell, return contact, and return reverse travel;
- action-rate, torque/work, fingertip-force proxy, fixed-base lateral force, and
  fixed-base bending moment as diagnostics only.

Checkpoint filenames such as `best_reward_*.pth` are not selectors. Evaluate
fixed candidate checkpoints with deterministic actions and a fixed episode
horizon. First discard every checkpoint that violates a hard gate; rank the
remaining candidates lexicographically by cycle success, net/gross efficiency,
pair contribution, and lower reverse travel. Episodic return is only a
diagnostic tie-breaker.

## 9. Required tests before cloud training

1. Unit-test forward, reverse, below-high-water, repeated milestone, reset, and
   mixed-environment traces. A `+a,-a,+a,-a` trace must not repeatedly earn
   progress and must have negative motion return after reverse cost.
2. Unit-test that unchanged double contact earns zero reward.
3. Unit-test same-step double release, one-step-asymmetric release, missing
   release, planned off-contact dwell, contact during return, and regrasp.
4. Unit-test every timeout as failure and prove it cannot increment transition
   or cycle-success counters.
5. Verify phase-aware termination permits planned release but still terminates
   workspace escape.
6. Run one-environment GPU smoke and inspect raw angle/contact/phase traces.
7. Run a small fixed-action diagnostic to verify the configured positive angle
   and signed-torque conventions.

### A0 failure recovery contract

The initial A0/A1 probe demonstrated that distance-only proximity was not a
stable grasp. The recovery is automated; the user is not required to operate
the interactive pose tuner.

1. Set `env.hand_init_dof_noise_fraction=0.0` for E002 so nominal means fixed.
2. Search bounded index/thumb offsets around the saved pose in parallel and
   require simultaneous force/distance contact for ten consecutive policy
   steps while the held nut drifts by no more than one degree.
3. From that fixed pose, probe independent index/thumb action vectors and both
   force-on-nut sign conventions. Accept only a candidate with at least five
   consecutive steps of simultaneous contact, positive commanded-axis torque
   from both fingers, and positive net nut angle.
4. Pass the selected pose and force sign explicitly to the smoke and PPO
   commands. Do not silently edit the historical M24 pose or E001 config.
5. In A1b, contact loss is physical pair-contact loss after acquisition; zero
   torque during a static hold is not contact loss. Static contact earns no
   recurring reward. Invalid/single-finger new progress is consumed and
   penalized, and cannot earn a delayed milestone.
6. Any A0b/A0c non-zero exit stops the pipeline before PPO. A healthy five-
   minute process is still not a behavior pass; apply the existing numeric and
   headed gates before any 30-minute extension or E002-B implementation.

For headed A1b evaluation, use the normal real-time viewer with
`M24_TASK_CONFIG=XHandPasiniM24NutBoltE002` and point
`E002_CALIBRATION_JSON` to the accepted pipeline's
`manifests/a0_calibration.json`. The viewer must refuse E002 without that file,
because the checkpoint requires the 101-D task plus its calibrated reset/sign.

## 12. Stop rules

Stop and diagnose instead of extending training when any of the following is
observed:

- reward rises while fixed-step net angle or cycle success does not;
- forward and reverse travel cancel;
- static contact contributes positive reward;
- either finger receives progress credit without the other contributing;
- a timeout increments a successful phase/cycle counter;
- missing releases appear as zero-lag releases;
- the actor depends on simulator-only contact or nut state not available in the
  intended deployment interface;
- headed playback disagrees with the numeric acceptance result.
